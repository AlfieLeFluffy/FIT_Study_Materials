Colour is complex, often subjective concept, that has many interpretations based on what context it is found in. 
## Definitions
There are several ways of defining colour based on the field of study/application:
### Physical
In the physical sense, colour is a light in a specific wave length range called the visible spectrum. Each different colour is a combination of light of different wave lengths, meaning that a colour such as the colour of a green birch leave can be composed of a large amount of mid range wave length light (500-600 nm) and two spikes (400nm, 700nm). The resulting colour is the integration over all visible wave lengths. 
Many other factors play into this such as what light absorbs the material that is being perceived and what light is being generated (pure white light, green light, blue light, etc).
### Biology
Into the perception of colour also plays the human eyes and experience as slight changes can lead to people perceiving colours differently then other and many animals can see outside this visible spectrum range and have more types of receptors then us, making them able to see colours we cannot.
### Computer
We represent colour in computers usually as a range of 3 primary colours (red, green, blue) and any other colour as a mixture of these base primaries. The range of these colours is usually from 0 to 255, but this depends on the format. This system is not entirely accurate to real world colours as there are many colours that are unpresentable in this system.
The basic colour spaces used in this system are:
- **HSL** (Hue, Saturation, Lightness)
- **HSV** (Hue, Saturation, Value)
### Printing
In printing, which is usually done on white paper, a different system of colour is used, in a way an inverse to the RGB system used in computers. CMYK (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Black) is used and the system of mixing is subtractive, not additive. The added black is for cost saving measures, as it reduces the need of mixing all colours together to create black, by just using black.
