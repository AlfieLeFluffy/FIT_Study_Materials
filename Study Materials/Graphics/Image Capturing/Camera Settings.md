There are many camera settings (setups) that influence the resulting image.
## Lens Assembly
The lens is responsible with focusing the incoming light through either optical lenses or mirrors onto the image sensor. There are many characteristics that can be adjusted, such as:
- Light Regulation
- Zoom 
- Sharpness
- Blur
These characteristics are most often influenced by **focal length**.
### Focal Length
Focal length is distance between the optical center of lenses and sensor. The main effect of this setting is changing the **FOV** (field of view) that the image captures. The relation between these values is inversely proportional, meaning that a large focal length creates a small FOV, while small focal length creates a large FOV.
A changeable focal length is also called **zoom**. 
### Aperture
An aperture is a part of the lens assembly that regulates the amount of incoming light that passes through it. Its main effect is creating a varying amount of depth of sharpness.
An aperture value is a ration of the **focal length** and a diameter of the aperture hole.
### Optical Defects
There are four main optical defects that can occur as a result of using specifically optical lenses. These defects are:
- **Chromatic Aberration** is a result of light decomposition into its wavelength components on the edges of a lens.
- **Spherical Aberration** is a result of light bending significantly more on the edges of of a lens.
- **Vignetting** is a lack of light intensity at the edges and corners of the captured image.
- **Ghosts/Flares** are the results of unintended/unwanted reflections inside the lens assembly. 
## Shutter
A shutter is the mechanism through which the camera captures incoming image onto the sensor. This can be done through either mechanics, such as in SLR (mirror) cameras or through electrics (sensor dependent). These sensors can be either:
- **Rolling** captures an image in segments (line, multiple lines) at one time.
- **Global** captures an whole image in one moment and then processes the input afterwards.
### Exposure Time
A duration of time in which the shutter is open and allows light to be captured on the sensor. The length of exposure time can drastically change the resulting image mainly for moving objects, where a moving object in a long exposure time will look smeared on the final image.
This values is usually measured in fractions of a second ($\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{1}{30}$, $\frac{1}{500}$).
## Sensitivity
Sensitivity, also commonly referred to as **ISO**, is the light sensitivity of the capturing material. Higher ISO means the material is more sensitive to light and captures the image faster. Digital cameras replicate the photosensitive materials/tape through amplification of the captured charge. This also introduces an issue with the natural noise a sensor has as it amplifies it as well.
