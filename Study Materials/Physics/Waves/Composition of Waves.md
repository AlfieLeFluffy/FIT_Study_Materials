We can compose waves of different **phase velocities**. For two harmonic waves $u_{1}$ and $u_{2}$ that are moving in the same direction, their angle frequency and wave vectors are relatively close, the wave functions are: $$u_{1}=\sin((\omega - \Delta \omega)t-(k-\Delta k)z)$$ $$u_{2}=\sin((\omega - \Delta \omega)t-(k-\Delta k)z)$$
Their composition creates a wave function: $$u=u_{1}+u_{2}=u_{mg}\sin(\omega t-kz)$$ where $u_{mg}=2 u_{m} \cos(\Delta \omega t-\Delta kz)$. We can find velocity $c$ with which the phase moves through $\phi=\omega t-kz$ of wave $u$. By differentiation $c=dz / dt= \omega / k$, which is the phase velocity.
The common definition of **group velocity** is: $$v_{\text{group}}=dz / dt = \sigma \omega / \sigma k$$
Through group velocity travels energy. Group velocity it is required to take into account during movement in dispersion environment.
