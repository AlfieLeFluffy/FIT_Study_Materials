We distinguish between several different types of waves in physics that share some common properties.
## Properties
We can divide waves into two groups by the way the wave travels through space:
- **Transverse waves** (shear, rotational)
	- The direction of the movement is perpendicular to the direction of the wave spreading. 
	- This for example occurs in electromagnetic waves.
	- With mechanical waves the object is reshaped by the wave.
- **Longitudinal waves** (dilatation)
	- The direction of movement of the elements is the same as the direction of the wave.
	- This for example occurs in sound waves in the atmosphere and in water.
	- With mechanical waves the object's density is changed as the waves compresses and stretches it.
We also have a wave-front division:
- **Ray** gives the direction in which a wave is spreading
- **Wave-fronts** are places with the same phase
Another division is between the direction of spreading from the origin:
- **Spherical** starts at one point in space and the waves spread in a spherical shape in all directions.
- **Parabolic** are the result of spherical waves traveling far enough to be considered parabolic.
- **Planar** are the result of parabolic waves traveling far enough from the point of origin that they are practically straight.
- There is no hard division between these three forms. Real planar waves are not straight, but their parabolic nature is so small that for us they might as well be.

## Types
Some of these wave types are:
### Mechanical
Mechanical waves move through material and elastic environments (solid matter, liquids, etc.) and do not spread through vacuum. These mechanical waves are satisfied by **Newton's laws**. There is a transfer of energy, momentum but not material is moved.
Examples of these waves are:
- Waves on top of water surfaces
- [[Sound]] waves
- Seismic waves
### Electromagnetic
Electromagnetic waves spread through both material environment but also vacuum. They are satisfied by **Maxwell's equations** and there is a transference of both energy and momentum.
Examples of such waves are:
- [[Wave Optics|Light]] (visible, ultraviolet) at $c=299792458 \ m/s=3\times 10^8 \ m/s$
- Radio
- Television
- Microwaves
- RTG devices
- Gamma
### Matter
Solid objects (electrons, protons, atoms, molecules, etc.) under certain circumstances behave like waves. We can assign to them wavelength, frequency, etc. These waves are satisfied by **Schrodinger's equations**. We can observe interference of elements, diffraction of elements, etc.
