The basic postulates of wave optics are:
- Light travels in the form of waves in vacuum at the speed $c=3\times10^8$.
- Homogenous transparent environment, such as glass, is characterised by a single constant. That constant is the **refraction index** $n$ ($n>1$). In an environment with refraction index $n$ moves light waves at a lower speed, which is $$c_{i}=\frac{c}{n}$$
- Mathematically the optical wave is described by a real function of position $r=(x,y,z)=xi+yj+zk$ and time $t$. This real function is described as $u(r,t)$ and is known as the **wave function**. It satisfies the wave equation $$\triangle u -\frac{1}{c_{i}^2} \frac{\sigma^2 u(\hat{r},t)}{\sigma t^2}=0$$ where the $\triangle$ is the Laplace operator.
- The princip of a **[[superposition]]** holds true because the wave equation is linear.
A lot of the basic ideas and mechanics of wave optics are described with normal [[Waves|wave physics]]. Additionally to that there are some light effects and properties that are also explained by wave optics such as [[Dispersion|dispersion]] and [[Interference|interference]].
