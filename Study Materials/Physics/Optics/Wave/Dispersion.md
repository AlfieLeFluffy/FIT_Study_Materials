Dispersion can be explained as dependency of spreading of **phase velocity** on its **wavelength**. Dispersion can be caused by geometric dimensions (thin plate, shallow water, etc.) or interactions of waves in solid environment in which the waves are spreading.
**Phase velocity** (refractory index) of any solid environment (not vacuum) depends on the wavelength of the light.
Dispersion occurs in case where parallel (planar) waves of differing wavelengths have different velocity of spreading. In other words, the wave ball of yarn or a pulse of differing wavelength have a tendency to disperse energy in space.
The dispersion equation is $c=c(\lambda)=\lambda.v(\lambda)$ with $c=c(k)$
Examples of dispersion in nature can be a rainbow that is caused by dispersion that partially dissolves white light into its components because of the differing wavelengths.
